# NeuroPD — Consolidated Findings Report

> Generated by `scripts/generate_report.py` from the committed, de-identified result bundles in `dashboard/data/`. Every number below is read from executed results. Regenerate after any analysis changes. Research tool only: nothing here is a diagnostic or prognostic claim.

## Question

Do interpretable resting-state EEG features that separate Parkinson's disease (PD) from controls in one cohort still work in independent cohorts? And where they do not, why not?

## Cohorts (after quality control)

| cohort | role | n | PD / control | mean age | % male |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ds007526 | development | 133 | 106 / 27 | 65.2 | 59% |
| ds002778 | external validation | 30 | 15 / 15 | 63.0 | 47% |
| ds004584 | confirmatory (pre-registered) | 133 | 89 / 44 | 69.3 | 64% |
| ds003490 | confirmatory (pre-registered) | 33 | 21 / 12 | 69.3 | 73% |

## Finding 1 — Site is far more predictable than disease

A logistic regression on the same EEG features tells which dataset a participant came from (ds007526 vs each other cohort, grouped CV) with ROC-AUC 0.953 (ds002778), 0.916 (ds004584), 0.924 (ds003490). Within ds007526, disease is predicted at ROC-AUC 0.718 (logreg, grouped CV) and age/sex alone reach 0.688. Site and acquisition dominate the shared feature space. (The ds002778 identity value and the within-ds007526 values come from the v0.1.0 analyses on the original 210-feature matrix; the others use the current 230 features.)

## Finding 2 — Disease signal transfers to some sites, not all

All-feature logistic regression trained on ds007526 only, evaluated once on each external cohort (ROC-AUC, 95% participant bootstrap CI):

| external cohort | n (PD) | all-feature logreg | age/sex only |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ds002778 | 30 (15) | 0.720 [0.520, 0.890] | 0.538 [0.321, 0.754] |
| ds004584 | 133 (89) | 0.857 [0.788, 0.916] | 0.462 [0.358, 0.573] |
| ds003490 | 33 (21) | 0.532 [0.308, 0.739] | 0.419 [0.196, 0.635] |

Transfer is clear for the largest new site, modest for ds002778 and not detectable for ds003490 (small, ~1 min of eyes-open EEG). Age/sex alone do not transfer anywhere, so the transferred signal is not a demographic proxy.

## Finding 3 — Harmonization alone does not fix transfer; site diversity may

Per-feature harmonization (per-cohort z-scoring, ComBat) removes site offsets but leaves single-site transfer essentially unchanged. Training on three sites and testing on the fourth (all-feature logreg, ROC-AUC):

| held-out site | ds007526 only | 3 sites pooled | 3 sites, per-site z |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ds007526 | - | 0.630 [0.495, 0.756] | 0.631 [0.503, 0.754] |
| ds002778 | 0.720 [0.520, 0.890] | 0.684 [0.472, 0.862] | 0.813 [0.636, 0.946] |
| ds004584 | 0.857 [0.788, 0.916] | 0.841 [0.768, 0.907] | 0.840 [0.767, 0.905] |
| ds003490 | 0.532 [0.308, 0.739] | 0.619 [0.388, 0.817] | 0.663 [0.435, 0.857] |

Per-site standardization combined with multi-site training tends to help the two small sites. This is directional (overlapping CIs), and the pre-declared all-sites criterion was not met.

## Finding 4 — Within PD, EEG slowing tracks cognition at two sites

Among PD participants only, a global theta/alpha ratio relates to lower MoCA after accounting for age: r = -0.260 (ds007526, n=103) and r = -0.287 (ds004584, n=89). This is a modest but replicated within-disease association. Occipital peak alpha frequency shows none.

## What did not hold up

- The exploratory claim that only *normalized* (relative) band power transfers (ADR 0010, from ds002778 with n=30) was not supported on ds004584, where every feature family transferred about equally.
- No harmonization method tested improved transfer consistently across sites.

## Main limitations

- Small external cohorts (n=30 and n=33) give wide intervals.
- ds007526 is imbalanced (about 4:1 PD:control) with an age/sex confound, and quality-control exclusions there are PD-concentrated.
- ds003490 has only about 1 minute of eyes-open EEG per session; its quality-control losses hit controls hardest.
- The ds004584 motor scale is undocumented, so motor-severity results are descriptive only.
- ds004584 and ds003490 share an author, so they are not fully independent.
